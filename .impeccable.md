# Design Context — PseudoCoding Portfolio

## Product
Single-page scroll portfolio for a Principal Software Engineer at PseudoCoding, LLC.

## Audience
Technical hiring managers, CTOs, engineering leadership. People who review 50+ portfolios — they are immune to template aesthetics and notice immediately when something is distinctive vs AI-generated.

## Brand Personality
Terminal/hacker — intentional and dark. Think Vercel, Raycast. Confident, precise, opinionated. The engineering craft IS the personality. No decorative fluff. Every detail should feel chosen, not generated.

## Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme**: Dark terminal / hacker — NOT "dark with glowing neon accents" (that's AI slop)
- **Accent**: Phosphor green (`#86e33d`) — desaturated lime/chartreuse. ONE color. No palette mixing.
- **Background**: True near-black `#0a0a0a` — zero blue tint (cold blue-black reads as "space theme AI portfolio")
- **Typography**: JetBrains Mono as an INTENTIONAL accent (code comments, labels, handles). Space Grotesk for body.
- **Surfaces**: Flat, opaque. Hard borders. No glassmorphism, no backdrop-blur, no rgba transparency on backgrounds.
- **Gradients**: None between two accent colors. Single-color gradients (light→dark of one hue) only if needed.
- **Shadows**: Subtle elevation shadows only. No colored glow halos.

## What Makes This Unforgettable
The `// PseudoCoding` nav wordmark, the `// eyebrow` section labels, and the JetBrains Mono handle in the hero should all feel like they belong to one coherent terminal aesthetic — not decoration layered over a generic template. The green accent is rare and confident.

## Hard Rules
- No `backdrop-blur` anywhere
- No `rgba()` for card/surface backgrounds — use solid opaque colors
- No two-color gradients (cyan-to-indigo, green-to-indigo, etc.)
- No `rounded-2xl` for cards — use `rounded-lg` max for crisper terminal feel
- No glow box-shadows on hover — use border color change + subtle shadow
- One accent color: `#86e33d` (and its shades). Nothing else.
